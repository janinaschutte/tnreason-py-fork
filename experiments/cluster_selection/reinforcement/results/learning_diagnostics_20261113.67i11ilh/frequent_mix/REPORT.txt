Frequent generic portfolio: root-only construction and attribution

The six generic sources rotate after every emitted proposal. Each affordable cluster is contracted under current domains, one temporary summary is used for GAC, then removed. Any pruning refreshes all proposal streams while preserving the next source in the rotation. No DFS, assignment or assumption is used.

The supervised mask removed the most cell values (378), made the most useful steps (25), and helped in the most roots (13). The teacher was close in pruning (361 values, 24 useful steps) and supplied the final step in the most solved roots (4). Q made 2,184 contractions with no pruning. These counts reflect this source order and the states produced by all preceding sources; they do not rank intrinsic model strength or assign sole credit for a solve. A useful step may prune auxiliary variables only; cell-value counts include subsequent full GAC.

Component time sums proposal generation, preparation and contraction/GAC time charged to that source. It excludes checkpoint/loop overhead. Full root times above include active-loop checkpoints, but exclude model/network loading, initial GAC and final validation. The expert is a separate Sudoku-specific reference with larger cluster limits, not a source in this generic portfolio. Times were recorded under concurrent CPU execution and are descriptive.

For each omission, the remaining original core/output sets were re-contracted in their saved order under the new current domains, starting from ordinary GAC. There were no new model queries, DFS calls or unaffordable retained clusters. This tests dependence of the recorded proof, not what a fresh adaptive policy would find without that source. Finishing moves are necessarily part of these finite recorded proofs. For example, the mixed proof for position 12 fails if mask, teacher, REINFORCE or replay clusters are omitted, although the earlier teacher-first run solved that puzzle independently. Therefore these omission losses do not prove those sources are necessary for solving the puzzle.
