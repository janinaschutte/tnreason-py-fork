Cluster construction-length diagnostic (existing validation only)

Question
Can shorter prefixes of the current REINFORCE construction already prune?

Setup
16 GAC-stalled puzzles selected by a fixed shuffle of the existing 55-puzzle
validation split, without using learned performance for selection. Four
constructions per puzzle: one greedy and three seeded samples. No training,
checkpoint changes, or inputs from the heldout100 evaluation set.

The original sampled core order was retained. Every prefix containing all
selected outputs was contracted independently under identical current domains,
followed by GAC and rollback. Each trial kept the usual 64-core, 6-output,
1M-work and 100K-entry bounds. A separate comparison retained only the original
anchor, with its own full-length comparator. Plain MAC then measured remaining
DFS nodes, without further clusters. Finish legality under the current cycle
mask was recorded; there were no rejected contractions or censored DFS solves.

Findings with the original outputs
64 constructions; 15 full clusters pruned. All 15 had a shorter legal useful
prefix. First usefulness appeared at 53-63 cores (median 57), versus 59-64
cores (median 63) for the useful full constructions.
14 of 15 reproduced exactly the full pruning at a strictly shorter prefix.
The earliest full-equivalent prefix saved a median four cores (including the
one case that needed the full construction). These matching prefixes retained
the same DFS result.
The first useful prefix had equal DFS nodes in 14 cases and worse DFS in one;
none improved DFS. In puzzle 238, draw 3, stopping at 56 cores removed 5 values
and left 8 DFS nodes; completing 63 cores removed 25 values and left 7 nodes.
Two full constructions solved their puzzle at the root, and their identical
pruning prefixes also solved it. These are isolated one-cluster applications,
not a complete multi-attempt solver benchmark.

Outputs and contraction cost
Retaining only the anchor was useful in 2/64 cases, compared with 15/64 for the
original outputs. Its two useful cases also had shorter matching prefixes.
Output selection therefore matters; restricting summaries to the anchor loses
most of the useful constructions in this sample.
Fewer cores did not always mean less estimated contraction work: in three
cases the earliest full-equivalent prefix had higher estimated work than the
full cluster. Each prefix was planned separately.

Interpretation
This supports testing modest late stopping, not a small-cluster restriction.
The complete trace and outputs were generated before testing prefixes. Timing
measurements are retrospective; timestamps to the last selected prefix core
exclude alternate stop/output decisions, so this is not a demonstrated solver
speedup. Single-trial contraction timings are noisy. The 64 constructions come
from 16 puzzles and must not be treated as 64 independent puzzle observations.
No shorter prefix improved pruning or DFS over its full fixed-output comparator.
Any learned stopping change needs a separate end-to-end validation experiment.

Verification
All 6029 trials retained an independently obtained valid baseline assignment.
Domains, cores, counters and queues were checked after every rollback. An
independent review checked split membership, all prefix lengths, monotonicity,
recomputed pruning/entropy, summaries and source hashes. Checkpoints and inputs
were unchanged. See protocol.json, summary.json and audit.json for exact records.
